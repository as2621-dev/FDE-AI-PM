# Residual review findings — Day 04 (reviewed at `86f7bd6`)

Three reviewers ran against Day 4. Every finding was applied except the four below, each
with the reason. Two of these are deliberate refusals, not backlog.

## 1. Day 3 carries the same fabricated claim, and I was not allowed to touch it

Reviewer 1 found that Day 4's §4 said *"the URL in `FDE_Report` has moved"* — and
`FDE_Report` contains **zero URLs** (`grep -c 'http' FDE_Report` → `0`). Its resource tables
name sources in prose only. Nothing has moved, because nothing was ever linked.

Fixed in Day 4. **Not fixed in Day 3**, which makes the same claim in two places
(`course/day-03-guardrails/README.md`, around lines 271 and 281). The slice's constraints
forbid modifying a Day 1–3 README, so this is filed rather than fixed.

Whoever picks this up: the underlying facts in Day 3 are fine — those docs sites genuinely
did move, and `docs/solutions/conventions/verify-a-source-moved-not-died.md` has the
evidence table. Only the attribution to `FDE_Report` is wrong. The fix is to say the source
moved, not that the report's link moved.

## 2. Refused: a magnitude for the LongMemEval result

Reviewer 2's strongest finding was that the day cites the *direction* of Chroma's
focused-vs-full result and never the *size*, so the reader cannot say whether 112,700
irrelevant tokens cost two accuracy points or forty.

The finding is correct. It is not fixable from the source: Chroma presents those results as
per-family charts, not as a table, and the report's prose gives no accuracy pair. Supplying a
number would mean reading values off a chart or inventing one, and a figure the reader
repeats in an interview must be quotable from a source.

What shipped instead: the gap is stated in the day, in the two-caveat paragraph after the
result — the direction is citable, the magnitude is not, and if a customer asks how much
worse, the answer is that it depends on their data and measuring it there is Week 3's job.
An honest gap the reader can work around beats a number he cannot defend.

**If someone wants to close this properly:** the raw results are in
`https://github.com/chroma-core/context-rot`, and a real figure computed from the released
data — with the computation shown — would be a genuine improvement over both options.

## 3. Refused in part: dropping the word "ceiling" for attention

Reviewer 3 argued that "ceiling" and "binds first" reinstate exactly the cliff that
Anthropic's *performance gradient* quote corrects, since a gradient has no line to cross.

The observation is right and the day now says so in prose, in a paragraph placed where
ceiling two is introduced rather than in the diagram footer where the correction had been
buried. The diagram's focal label changed from `CEILING 2 — attention` to
`ATTENTION — a slope, not a line`.

The word itself stays. Day 2 closes with *"Attention binds before the window does in both
cases, and that's Day 4's"*, so the course has already committed to this framing, and a
reader holding both days needs one vocabulary rather than a silent correction. Naming the
imprecision inside the day is the right fix; forking the terminology from the day that
handed the topic over is not.

## 4. Prompt caching: named, not costed

Reviewer 2 noted that caching is the first thing an interviewer raises against
"put the manual in every request", and that it appears nowhere in Days 1–4.

Day 4 now names it in one sentence — providers cache repeated prompt prefixes, which removes
the cost objection but not the accuracy one — and defers the economics. `DAY_MAP.md` assigns
"prompt caching economics" to Day 19, and quantifying it here would cross that boundary.
**Day 19 should know the reader has met the word and nothing more.**

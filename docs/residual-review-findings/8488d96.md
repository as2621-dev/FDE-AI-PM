### 1. Day 2 and Day 4 contradict each other on the units of 19,700 tokens

`course/day-02-tool-use/README.md`, near line 173:

> On Haiku 4.5's 200,000-token window the same sum gives **19,700 per result**

`course/day-04-context-and-memory/README.md`, near line 111:

> Day 2's 19,700 came from dividing the window across ten passes, making it a per-***pass***
> allowance, not a per-***result*** one

**Day 4 is right.** Day 2's own method is `(window − base prompt) ÷ passes`, so the quotient is
denominated per pass; the label "per result" on Day 2's page is the defect.
`docs/solutions/conventions/derived-knobs-must-be-derived-together.md` records the resolution,
and Day 4's review panel found it independently. Two of Day 5's three reviewers flagged the
inconsistency between Day 5 and Day 2.

Day 5 now states the per-pass reading, attributes the correction to Day 4 in a parenthesis, and
does not silently average the two. **Day 2's sentence still needs the one-word fix**, and it is
the higher-leverage place to make it, since a reader meets Day 2 first.

passed along). A dedicated §6 row, and the W3C `traceparent` header that carries it, were cut.
General distributed-tracing plumbing is thin value for a reading-only PM against its word cost.

## Refused, with reasons

### 7. A specific `decision_wait` value for tail-based sampling

Reviewer 2 asked for the OpenTelemetry Collector's tail-sampling decision window, characterised
as "tens of seconds". The point is right and Day 5 makes it — "the buffer has a time limit that
agent runs can outlast" — **without a number**, because I did not fetch the Collector's
documentation and will not quote a default from memory. A reader who repeats an invented default
in a customer meeting is exactly the harm the sourcing rules exist to prevent.

### 8. A full derivation for *k*, the number of captured examples wanted

Reviewer 1 held that `k = 5` is asserted, and that since it sets the 25% answer it owes a method.
Under Rule B as amended by `38fe1ed` — subject knobs owe a full method, input knobs owe enough to
pick and defend — *k* is an input, and it is the one input in the sum that is a judgment rather
than a measurement. Day 5 now says exactly that, and says which way to move it ("if you need to
be confident of five, ask for ten"). Adding a derivation for a judgment call would manufacture
false precision, which is the trap
`docs/solutions/conventions/verify-a-source-moved-not-died.md` and Day 2's review both warn about.

### 9. An in-text justification for landing in the 9,000–10,000 word band

Reviewer 1 noted the day carries no in-text justification for the overflow. The style guide says
the writer owes the *reader* a reason, and Day 4 established the precedent of putting that
reasoning in the day report rather than spending reader-facing words on it. Kept in
`.agents/day-reports/day-05.md`.
